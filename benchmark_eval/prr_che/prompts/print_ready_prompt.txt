You are evaluating whether a rendered scientific poster is ready for direct public display at an academic conference.

A poster is PRINT-READY only when the supplied full render functions as a finished display artifact. It must communicate the work at poster scale and must not require converting a prose-led handout, completing an underfilled layout, restoring overflowed content, replacing failed elements, or repairing prominent corrupted text.

Required communication:
- A visible paper title or equally clear work identifier
- Author names and at least one visible author-affiliation identifier
- Substantive scientific content
- A meaningful main method or approach
- Experimental evidence, results, or a work-specific explicit finding

An affiliation identifier may be readable institution/laboratory/company/organization text, a named institutional wordmark, or a conventional institutional-style academic seal or crest placed adjacent to the author line. Conference branding, generic icons, project logos, and ordinary abstract branding do not establish author affiliation.

Inspect in this order:
1. Inspect the full canvas for balance, unusually empty intended body regions, content mis-scaled inside large assigned panels, and content continuing outside the render.
2. Inspect the title/author band for title, author names, an affiliation identifier, overlap, raw syntax, unrelated inserted fragments, or malformed identity text.
3. Trace top, right, bottom, and left edges. Check every column ending, heading, figure, table, and continuing sentence.
4. Scan the body as a 3-by-3 grid. Count overlaps, blank/black failed regions, placeholders, and abrupt section endings.
5. Inspect the main method and findings. Judge the usable area and communication role of scientific visuals.

Complete every field before applying the hard mapping.

1. Author affiliation presence
- `present`: either (a) at least one readable institution, laboratory, company, or organization name is visibly associated with the author line, or (b) a named institutional wordmark or conventional institutional-style academic seal/crest is placed adjacent to the authors.
- `missing_or_unrelated_branding_only`: no author affiliation identifier is visible; the header has author names alone, or only a conference/venue mark, generic icon, project logo, or unrelated branding.

A conventional academic seal/crest next to the authors counts even without a separate affiliation sentence or readable institution name. Its internal microtext and exact institution name need not be readable when the emblem visibly has institutional seal/crest structure, such as a shield, laurel, motto ring, or university-style heraldic mark, and its placement clearly associates it with the author line. A named company/university wordmark also counts. Do not treat a venue mark, generic icon, project logo, or ordinary abstract logo as a seal merely because it is near the header. Do not infer affiliation from an author email domain or project URL. An AAAI, CVPR, ICML, or similar venue mark is not an author affiliation.

2. Display mode
- `poster_first`: large scientific visuals, concise findings, diagrams, plots, tables, or qualitative comparisons carry most communication.
- `mixed_poster`: prose and poster-scale scientific visuals both make substantial contributions without a sustained paragraph-led reading path dominating the body.
- `paper_like_handout`: sustained reading dominates. Use this when either (a) continuous paragraph prose occupies about half or more of the body, or (b) the explicit panel counts satisfy P >= 5 and P + B >= 6.

For every artifact, count `P`, the number of distinct semantic panels led by a continuous prose paragraph of about five or more lines, and `B`, the number led by multiple long, wrapped, explanatory full-sentence bullets. Count each labeled or unlabeled semantic panel once. Never count one wide or tall text block again merely because it crosses multiple cells of the 3-by-3 inspection grid.

Do not count a compact caption, equation block, table, metric callout, result card, or concise poster-scale bullet list merely because lines wrap. One long bullet is not enough for B. Apply the arithmetic condition exactly: when P >= 5 and P + B >= 6, use `paper_like_handout` even if several display-scale visuals exist. Scientific visuals do not override that condition. Long prose bullets contribute through B but do not independently satisfy the separate half-body area route. Output P and B as integers in the required JSON.

3. Primary visual evidence scale
- `display_scale`: one usable method/evidence visual occupies about one-fifth or more of the body, or multiple usable scientific visuals collectively occupy about two-fifths or more and directly communicate core relationships or findings.
- `supporting_scale`: visuals exist but are smaller, secondary, sparse, embedded as illustrations, or dependent on prose.
- `absent`: no meaningful scientific visual is present.

Apply the area thresholds. This field is diagnostic and does not override `paper_like_handout`.

4. Scientific coverage
- `complete`: a meaningful method plus evidence, results, or a work-specific explicit finding are visibly communicated.
- `thin`: method or findings are generic template labels, decorative icons, or sparse statements that do not say what this work does or found.
- `missing`: work identity, substantive content, method, or any result/finding is absent.

5. Evidence specificity
- `demonstrated`: a plot, table, qualitative example, ablation, named comparison, metric context, or interpretable result is visible.
- `claim_level`: a work-specific result or finding is explicitly stated, but independent visible support is limited.
- `absent`: no result or finding is visible.

`claim_level` is not a blocker by itself. Do not mark coverage thin merely because a specific finding lacks a plot or table.

6. Overview legibility
- `usable`: the main method relationship and headline finding are understandable in the full render.
- `marginal`: supporting details are small, but the main method and finding remain usable.
- `unusable`: understanding depends predominantly on tiny or illegible content.

7. Canvas-edge completeness
- `complete`: no meaningful content is cut by an outer edge.
- `minor_ancillary`: only a citation, footnote, decorative mark, or other nonessential fragment crosses an edge.
- `major_truncation`: an edge cuts a heading, multiple lines, an entire continuing paragraph or substantive sentence, a figure/table region, or a visibly continuing section.

8. Layout completion
- `finished`: body regions have intentional endpoints; normal margins and deliberate balanced whitespace are allowed.
- `underfilled_or_overflowing`: roughly a lower quarter or a substantial intended column is conspicuously blank while neighboring content ends abruptly, a major section continues beyond the canvas, or the composition plainly looks partially populated.

Do not use underfilled for balanced minimalist layouts or ordinary whitespace between complete panels.

9. Body fill integrity
- `well_fitted`: content is reasonably scaled within its assigned panels and no severe unassigned body void remains.
- `severely_underfilled_or_misscaled`: either (a) one contiguous unassigned blank area occupies roughly one-sixth or more of the usable body, or (b) a principal panel occupies about one-third or more of the body while its actual diagram, text, or evidence is confined to about half or less of that panel, leaving the rest conspicuously unused.

Judge panel occupancy, not the amount of white background inside a normally scaled sparse diagram. Normal outer margins, gutters, balanced whitespace around complete panels, a deliberate footer, and symmetric whitespace are `well_fitted`. If the severe state applies, do not excuse it merely because the small content has a clean endpoint.

10. Critical-region integrity
- `intact`: title/work identity, main method, and findings remain usable.
- `damaged`: clipping, overlap, blank/black failure, corruption, or malformed content destroys an essential identity, method relationship, result comparison, table, or prominent scientific visual.

11. Collision scope
- `none`: no meaningful overlap or crop is visible.
- `isolated`: exactly one local caption, label, or ordinary sentence is interrupted while the section and critical content remain understandable.
- `repeated_or_section_level`: two or more distinct content regions are cut or covered, a heading covers its section, or one collision removes a substantial region.

12. Header and final-state integrity
- `clean`: no unfinished or corrupted display material remains.
- `local_quirk`: one non-obscuring guide, short ancillary production note, isolated numbering/Markdown marker, minor typo, or harmless malformed fragment remains without hiding content or indicating required content is absent.
- `critical_or_repeated`: unresolved placeholders, instructions for missing content, raw code/LaTeX or unrelated inserted identity/heading text, repeated semantic corruption, sustained gibberish, obscuring editing/debug state, or failed figures/important regions remain.

A single `##` fragment, short production note, or guide outline is not critical when required content is present.
